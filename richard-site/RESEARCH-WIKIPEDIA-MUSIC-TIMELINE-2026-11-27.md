# Richard Morton website: source-grounded biography, timeline and music research

Research compiled 27 November 2026. Nothing in this brief is permission to publish.

## Executive editorial recommendation

The site should communicate three overlapping careers: (1) Richard as an award-winning live comic and alternative-comedy contemporary, (2) Richard as a musician and member of The Panic Brothers, and (3) Richard as a composer, broadcaster and maker of retro-inspired instrumentals. The present site leads with stand-up but gives his music too little explanatory space. Treat music and radio as a meaningful content feature, not merely as three album-cover images.

Rather than rewriting Wikipedia or constructing a long traditional biography, use original prose and source-linked milestone captions. His own two recent Facebook screenshots are especially valuable because they contain first-person context and current news beyond the Wikipedia article.

## Source hierarchy and editorial safeguards

1. **Richard's own material:** the email headed "Richard Morton Website" received 23 November 2026 included two YouTube clip links, three BBC radio programme links, the Wikipedia URL, and two inline social-post screenshots. The posts cover theme music used on BBC radio and Bennett Arron's November 2026 programme. These are user-supplied source material, not yet final article copy. Do not paste the screenshots' full prose into the published site without editing/approval.
2. **University of Kent Special Collections catalogue**: an unusually good independent source. BSUCA/RM describes 97 physical items (1987–2001), chronologically arranged, including flyers, programmes, posters and photos. It confirms 1985 Panic Brothers formation, the 1992 Time Out award and 1994 headline tour. Individual catalogue item BSUCA/RM/1/1 records 1987 Panic Brothers flyers and Edinburgh material.
3. **British Comedy Guide:** specialist credit-by-year listings; relevant to 1988, 1998, 2014 and 2021, as well as the theme credit for You Heard It Here First.
4. **Wikipedia:** a broad editorial index with citations to books, contemporary press, record retailers and BBC programmes. It is not a substitute for checking original images or unambiguous release dates.
5. **The 39 archive images:** Richard's downloaded source images now have web-sized private copies. Filenames indicate candidate eras; specific event dates and usage rights should be confirmed from the visible artifact and Richard. These are NOT in the public site.

**Rights:** Kent explicitly says collection items may remain in copyright and that users must obtain reproduction permissions beyond research/private study. Richard supplied scans for his website, which is helpful, but he should still confirm his right to publish each image. Link to BBC programme pages or embed Richard-approved original YouTube uploads, rather than copying BBC audio or rehosting recordings.

## Wikipedia narrative, distilled by theme

**Early life and influences:** Wikipedia says Richard was born in Singapore, grew up around Newcastle and Hexham, and played rock bands before heading to London in the late 1970s. Suitable for two sentences in a biography only if Richard likes that personal emphasis. The turning point was live banter overtaking his songs.

**The Panic Brothers:** formed in 1985 with Reg Meuross; close harmonies, sharp songs and acoustic guitars. London pub circuit, Edinburgh, Friday Night Live, mini-LP In The Red (1987), separation around 1990 and a 2014 reunion. This is visually rich, and a strong opening chapter for the timeline.

**Stand-up career:** a founder of the Comedy Store's Cutting Edge; 1992 Time Out Comedy Award; long Edinburgh record; own 1994 headline UK tour; support dates with Jack Dee, Lee Evans and Jo Brand; Channel 5 Comedy Store television and other broadcast appearances. Archive posters let us show this story rather than make a long credit list.

**Television and radio performance:** Wikipedia includes The Jack Dee Show, Our Friends in the North, Jack & Jeremy's Real Lives/Police 4, Radio 4 Loose Ends and Just A Minute, Stand Up 2 and Channel 5 appearances. Prefer named, dated, independently verifiable highlights. Avoid presenting the 1995 Our Friends in the North production as the 1995 transmission date.

**The Rich Morton Sound:** Wikipedia credits Richard with forming the project with musician/producer Dave O'Brien around 2010. The Theme That Never Was is a collection of freshly composed fictional TV/film themes in 1960s/70s styles; The Sequel That Never Was followed. The origin of Colour Me Groovy as a knowingly imaginary theme that became an actual programme signature tune provides an unusually good story for the site.

**Composition and broadcast documentaries:** credited with themes for Vic Reeves' Does the Team Think...? and several BBC programmes. Richard wrote/presented BBC Radio 4 documentaries on lounge music, stand-up and classic TV themes, including In The Lounge With Rich Morton, I Can't Stand Up for Falling Down and The Lost Art of the TV Theme.

**2026 activity:** Richard reports the use of Colour Me Groovy on Chris McCausland's You Heard It Here First, including a third series by March 2026, and a NEW theme for Bennett Arron's Big Welsh Con in November 2026. The BBC Wales programme exists in contemporary schedules. These warrant Latest News content: they demonstrate Richard is still creating work.

## Recommended information architecture

**Homepage:** keep compact portrait/intro and a strong watch or listen entrypoint; use a few meaningful career highlights, not a wall of programme logos.

**Music and Radio section:** split into three short visual areas: The Panic Brothers (history and music), The Rich Morton Sound (album covers and two supplied YouTube clips), and On the Radio (three BBC documentaries plus Colour Me Groovy's programme use). On mobile, use accessible expandable cards and avoid autoplay. This should become a reusable audio/video/media feature in the comedian website builder.

**Career timeline:** curate around 12–16 milestones that have good image evidence, plus major milestones without available photos only where we deliberately choose an appropriate presentation. Keep the timeline module switchable. Start as drafts and confirm dates/captions with Richard before publishing. University of Kent's chronologically curated collection is a valuable source and deserves a link.

**Latest News:** create two drafts: (1) how an imaginary theme became a real BBC signature tune, then Chris McCausland's show; (2) the November 2026 Bennett Arron theme. A third story could cover Richard's donation to the Kent archive. News should read in Richard's voice once he signs off; it should not be pasted Wikipedia prose.

**About:** add one or two original paragraphs that show his combined comedy/music/broadcast work. Avoid a long chronological résumé because the timeline will do that job.

## Proposed short original site copy for review, not publication

*Music and comedy:* "Richard started out with a guitar, long before the comedy took over. With Reg Meuross he formed The Panic Brothers, mixing close harmonies and sharp comic writing on club stages and at the Edinburgh Fringe. His later instrumental project, The Rich Morton Sound, brought the sounds of fictional television and film themes vividly to life."

*Radio and composition:* "The funny thing about inventing television themes for programmes that never existed is that one of them eventually found a real audience. Colour Me Groovy became theme music for comedy programmes on BBC Radio Wales and Radio 4. Richard has also written and presented documentaries exploring the music and the performers who shaped British entertainment."

*Archive:* "Decades of flyers, posters, photographs and festival programmes chart Richard's journey through British alternative comedy. A substantial collection of this material is preserved by the University of Kent's British Stand-Up Comedy Archive. Here are some of the moments behind the memorabilia."
